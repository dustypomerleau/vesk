Laser Eye Surgery /Laser vision correction

Laser eye surgery is a form of vision correction that involves using a cool-temperature laser to reshape the cornea to correct a refractive error. Laser eye surgery is also referred to as laser refractive surgery. A refractive error occurs when the eye is abnormally shaped and cannot focus light directly on the retina in the back of the eye. As a result, images appear out of focus giving blurry vision.

Examples of refractive error include short-sightedness (myopia), long-sightedness (hyperopia), age-related loss of reading vision (presbyopia) and astigmatism. All of these refractive problems can be corrected with prescription glasses or contact lenses. There can be downsides to prescription glasses or contact lenses. People with high refractive error or high prescription will have thick or heavy glasses which can be cumbersome and uncomfortable. High prescription glasses cause distortion of images especially towards the peripheral vision and can cause restriction in peripheral vision. Some patients are unable to use multifocal glasses due to distortion in the transition zones between distance and near viewing areas within the lens. Contact lens can be uncomfortable or intolerable in people with dry eyes. They can be associated with an increased risk of eye infection and may not be safe for people who are unable to maintain high contact lens hygiene, or tend to forget to remove them or sleep in them, Laser eye surgery is an alternative option to prescription glasses/contact lens.

LASIK eye surgery

LASIK is one of the most commonly performed laser vision correction procedures. It is designed to correct refractive error by changing the cornea’s shape in a controlled way. The laser treatment reshapes the cornea (the clear front window of the eye) to help light focus more accurately on the retina.

How is it performed?

The surgeon uses a laser to create a flap in the top layer of the cornea, exposing the tissue bed underneath. An excimer laser is used to precisely shape the corneal tissue bed to correct the patient’s refractive error. Finally the surgery places the corneal flap back in its original position. Because the flap adheres to the corneal bed, the healing process begins immediately. Patients are able to return to their most of their daily activities the next day.

What can LASIK correct:

Myopia (short-sightedness)

Hyperopia (long-sightedness)

Astigmatism

Candidates for LASIK

Suitability is based on eye health and measurements, not just your prescription. The assessment is designed to confirm that LASIK can be done safely and that the likely outcome matches your goals.

Basic eligibility factors include:

Age over 20 years

Stable prescription over last 12 months

Healthy eyes overall, including the cornea and ocular surface

A cornea with suitable thickness and shape for LASIK planning

Realistic expectations about what LASIK can and cannot achieve

Corneal thickness and corneal mapping

Corneal thickness is important as this determines whether there is adequate corneal tissue to be shaped to provide the vision correction required. Higher refractive correction will require more corneal tissue or thicker cornea for laser treatment.

Shape and stability are other factors that are also taken into consideration. Corneal mapping (tomography) checks for irregular patterns and subtle signs of corneal weakness. This screening helps reduce the risk of long term complications such as ectasia and helps identify people who may be safer with a different approach or a non-laser option.

LASIK may not be recommended in situations such as:

Increased risk of corneal instability, including signs of keratoconus or suspicious corneal mapping

Significant ocular surface disease that needs treatment first

Unstable refraction

Certain medical factors that may affect healing

If corneal stability is a concern, your surgeon may discuss corneal monitoring and, in appropriate situations, options like corneal cross-linking to help stabilise progressive corneal conditions.

LASIK Visual Outcomes & Long-Term Stability

LASIK aims to reduce dependence on glasses or contact lenses, but outcomes can vary and long-term vision is influenced by natural ageing changes.

Most people notice significant improvements, often within the first few days. Vision can continue to sharpen and stabilise over the following weeks as dryness settles and the cornea heals.

Some people may still need reading glasses for certain tasks, this is more likely if you are over the age of 40. If you later develop presbyopia, reading glasses may be needed even if distance vision remains clear. If your prescription changes over time, enhancement may be considered in selected cases, but only after stability and corneal safety are confirmed.

LASIK is designed to be long-lasting for the treated prescription. However, vision can still change over time due to normal ageing, presbyopia, and other eye health factors. Ongoing eye checks remain important even after surgery.

PRK (ASLA) eye surgery

Photorefractive Keratectomy (PRK) is a surface-based laser vision correction procedure that reshapes the cornea to reduce or eliminate your reliance on glasses or contact lenses. It is an excellent alternative to LASIK, particularly for patients with thinner corneas, irregular surface topography, or lifestyle and occupational factors where avoiding a corneal flap is preferred.

How is it performed?

Unlike LASIK, which involves creating a thin flap in the corneal tissue, PRK works directly on the front surface of the eye:

Epithelial Removal & Reshaping: The outermost protective layer of the cornea (the epithelium) is gently removed, allowing the excimer laser to precisely reshape the underlying corneal curvature to correct your refractive error.

Bandage Contact Lens Placement: Once the laser treatment is complete, a protective soft bandage contact lens is applied for 4-5 days to aid the healing process. This helps to shield the surface and optimize comfort during the early healing phase.

Surface Recovery & Visual Settlement: Over the following 3 to 5 days, the surface epithelial cells naturally regenerate beneath the lens. Visual acuity improves progressively as the surface smooths and stabilizes over the subsequent weeks.

What can PRK correct

PRK can be used to correct common refractive errors, including:

Myopia (short-sightedness)

Hyperopia (long-sightedness)

Astigmatism

Your exact suitability depends on prescription range, corneal measurements, and overall eye health.

Candidates for PRK

Photorefractive Keratectomy offers an effective route to visual independence for patients who may not be suitable candidates for flap-based procedures. PRK is commonly recommended for:

Thinner Corneas: Patients with thinner corneal tissue, where creating a LASIK flap could compromise structural integrity.

High-Impact Occupations & Sports: Individuals in contact sports, martial arts, military roles, or high-risk jobs where avoiding a corneal flap eliminates any potential risk of flap trauma or dislocation.

Specific Corneal Topographies: Unique corneal shape profiles where a surface-based approach provides a safer, more predictable visual outcome.

Corneal Health & Safety Screening

PRK is not appropriate for every eye. If pre-operative mapping identifies underlying corneal weakness or structural instability, surface treatment may also carry risk. Comprehensive screening for keratoconus and ectasia risk is a fundamental part of responsible refractive planning to ensure long-term ocular health and optimal visual results.

PRK Visual Outcomes & Long-Term Stability

Photorefractive Keratectomy delivers excellent, long-lasting visual outcomes for appropriately selected candidates. Understanding what to expect during recovery and as your eyes age helps ensure a smooth treatment journey:

The majority of patients achieve crisp distance vision following PRK. However, some individuals may still require spectacles for specialized visual tasks or fine near work, particularly as presbyopia naturally develops later in life.

PRK results are highly stable over time. It is important to note that natural age-related changes—such as presbyopia, which affects close-up focusing—will still occur. These normal age-related shifts are independent of the procedure and do not indicate a regression or failure of the initial treatment.

If additional fine-tuning is needed, enhancement procedures are considered only after the cornea has fully healed and the refractive outcome has completely stabilized. Careful timing is critical, as treating too early can compromise predictability and increase surgical risk.

SMILE pro eye surgery

SMILE pro (Small Incision Lenticule Extraction) is a next-generation, keyhole laser vision correction procedure designed to treat short-sightedness (myopia) and astigmatism. Utilizing advanced femtosecond laser technology (such as the VisuMax 800), it reshapes the cornea through a micro-incision—without the need for a corneal flap—delivering clear, natural vision with minimal disruption to the eye's surface.

How is SMILE pro performed?

Lenticule Creation: The femtosecond laser operates with extreme speed and precision, creating a microscopic, lens-shaped piece of tissue (the lenticule) within the intact corneal stroma in just 8 to 10 seconds per eye.

Keyhole Extraction: The surgeon gently extracts the lenticule through a tiny keyhole incision (typically less than 2 to 4 mm wide) on the corneal surface.

Corneal Preservation: Removing the lenticule reshapes the cornea to correct the refractive error. Because no flap is cut, SMILE pro preserves more of the cornea’s natural biomechanical strength and structural integrity compared to traditional LASIK, while significantly reducing the incidence of postoperative dry eye syndrome.

What can SMILE laser surgery correct

SMILE pro SmartSight can be used to correct common refractive errors, including:

Myopia (short-sightedness)

Astigmatism

Your exact suitability depends on prescription range, corneal measurements, and overall eye health.

Candidates for SMILE pro

SMILE pro is an ideal choice for patients seeking a fast, minimally invasive alternative to traditional laser procedures. You may be a suitable candidate if you:

Are at least 18 years old with a stable optical prescription for at least 12 months.

Have short-sightedness (myopia), with or without astigmatism.

Have healthy eyes with adequate corneal thickness and no underlying corneal disease.

A comprehensive pre-operative eye examination is essential to confirm your eligibility and ensure your safety.

When SMILE pro May Not Be Suitable

SMILE pro may not be recommended if you present with:

Hyperopia (Long-Sightedness): SMILE pro is currently designed exclusively for myopic and astigmatic corrections.

Very High Myopia: Severe short-sightedness where an Implantable Collamer Lens (ICL) offers superior visual quality and safety.

Corneal Irregularities: Conditions such as keratoconus or significant corneal ectasia.

Unstable Health & Surface: Severe active dry eye syndrome or a fluctuating visual prescription.

If SMILE pro is not the best fit for your eyes, alternative procedures such as LASIK, PRK, or ICL implantation will be discussed to tailor the safest solution for your visual goals.

Benefits of SMILE pro

SMILE pro offers a minimally invasive, flap-free approach to vision correction, providing several key advantages over traditional laser procedures:

Flap-Free Technology: Because no corneal flap is created, the risk of flap-related complications—such as displacement, dislocation, or epithelial ingrowth—is completely eliminated.

Micro-Incision Design: A tiny keyhole entry (just 2 to 4 mm) preserves the structural integrity of the cornea, supporting faster tissue recovery and greater biomechanical stability.

Lower Risk of Dry Eye: By avoiding a circumferential flap cut, SMILE pro preserves significantly more corneal nerve fibers, reducing the incidence and severity of postoperative dry eye symptoms.

Rapid Visual Recovery: The minimally invasive surface approach allows most patients to notice significant visual improvement within 24 to 48 hours.

Proven Precision: Advanced femtosecond laser technology delivers exceptional accuracy, optimizing predictable outcomes for myopia and astigmatism.

SMILE pro Visual Outcomes & Long-Term Stability

SMILE pro delivers exceptional, highly predictable visual outcomes with long-term stability for patients with short-sightedness and astigmatism. Understanding what to expect during recovery and as your eyes naturally age helps ensure a smooth, confident treatment journey:

Visual Recovery & Clarity: Visual recovery following SMILE pro is exceptionally swift. Most patients experience functional, clear vision within 24 to 48 hours, with visual acuity continuing to refine and sharpen over the following weeks as the keyhole incision fully settles.

Long-Term Stability: The refractive correction achieved with SMILE pro is permanent and highly stable over time. Because the procedure preserves more of the cornea's natural biomechanical strength, the structural baseline of your visual result remains secure.

Natural Ageing & Presbyopia: While SMILE pro permanently corrects your distance vision, it does not stop the natural ageing process of the eye. As you reach your mid-to-late 40s, you will naturally develop presbyopia—an age-related loss of close-focusing flexibility. This is a normal physiological shift that affects all eyes and does not indicate a regression or loss of your initial laser correction.

Enhancements After SMILE pro: In the rare event that fine-tuning is required, enhancement options are carefully evaluated once the corneal tissue has fully healed and the visual outcome has stabilized. Depending on your corneal profile, post-SMILE pro enhancements are typically performed using surface-based laser techniques (PRK) to maintain corneal safety and structural integrity.

PresbyMAX/PRESBYOND laser surgery.

PresbyMAX and PRESBYOND are specialized laser vision correction procedures designed to treat presbyopia—the age-related loss of near focusing ability that typically begins in one's 40s.

While traditional LASIK or PRK targets distance vision, both PresbyMAX and PRESBYOND reshape the cornea to restore a functional range of vision (near, intermediate, and distance), reducing reliance on reading glasses. However, they use fundamentally different optical strategies and laser platforms to achieve this.

PresbyMAX laser surgery:

uses laser reshaping to create a controlled multifocal corneal profile. The laser reshapes the central and peripheral regions of the cornea into distinct focus zones (similar in concept to a multifocal contact lens or intraocular lens). The center of the cornea is typically optimized for near/intermediate tasks, while the outer area provides distance vision (or vice versa, depending on the software profile chosen). As a result, both eyes receive a multifocal treatment, allowing each eye to contribute to distance, intermediate, and near vision simultaneously.

Key advantage: Provides strong near-vision performance and intermediate clarity without relying heavily on traditional binocular monovision.

Trade-Offs: The introduction of multiple focal zones on the cornea can sometimes cause mild visual phenomena, such as halos, glare around lights at night,

or a slight reduction in contrast sensitivity.

PRESBYOND (Laser Blended Vision)

PRESBYOND utilizes an extended depth of focus (EDOF) blended vision profile.

How It Works: Rather than creating distinct multifocal rings on the cornea, PRESBYOND alters the corneal spherical aberrations to extend the depth of field in each eye. One eye is set slightly more for distance, and the non-dominant eye is set slightly more for near. The extended depth of focus creates a "blend zone" where the image overlap is smooth.

Visual Experience: Instead of a harsh jump between distance and near (like traditional monovision), the brain easily merges the images from both eyes into a continuous visual field.

Key Advantage: Preserves higher contrast sensitivity, offers excellent binocularity, and tends to produce fewer night-vision disturbances (glare/halos) than multifocal profiles.

Trade-Offs: Requires neuroadaptation as the brain adjusts to the micro-monovision setup. Extremely fine print in dim lighting may still occasionally require light readers.

Candidate Suitability for PresbyMAX/PRESBYOND

Both options are generally suitable for adults aged 40 to 65 who:

Have presbyopia with underlying hyperopia, myopia, or astigmatism.

Have a healthy cornea (adequate thickness, no keratoconus or significant scarring).

Are free from progressive lens changes or cataracts. If early cataracts are present, Refractive Lens Exchange / RLE is often preferred over corneal laser surgery.

Visual Outcomes & Long-Term Considerations: PresbyMAX & PRESBYOND

Corneal laser options for presbyopia—such as PresbyMAX and PRESBYOND—are designed to expand your range of clear focus, reducing your daily dependence on reading glasses. Understanding how visual adaptation works and what to expect over time helps ensure a successful, well-informed result:

Primary Objective: The main goal is functional visual independence for everyday tasks, including mobile screen use, computer work, and general reading. While dependence on readers is significantly reduced, lightweight glasses may still be helpful for prolonged fine print reading or visual tasks in dim lighting.

Visual Profile & Trade-Offs:

PresbyMAX (Multifocal Profile): Creates distinct focus zones across the cornea. While it provides robust near and intermediate vision, distance vision may feel slightly softer during initial adaptation compared to standard monofocal treatments. Some patients may also notice mild glare or halos around light sources at night.

PRESBYOND (Laser Blended Vision): Combines an extended depth of focus (EDOF) with micro-monovision, creating a smooth blend zone between eyes. This approach preserves higher contrast sensitivity and generally produces fewer night-vision disturbances, though it requires brief neuroadaptation as the brain learns to merge the images.

Long-Term Considerations & Progression: Presbyopia is a dynamic, progressive age-related process that continues over time. While treatment gains are long-lasting, natural ocular aging continues. In future years, if your close-focusing needs shift or if natural age-related lens changes (cataracts) eventually develop, further options—such as touch-up laser enhancements or custom lens replacement—can be evaluated based on your overall eye health.

Implantable Collamer Lens (ICL)

An Implantable Collamer Lens (ICL) is a specialized, biocompatible lens implanted inside the eye to correct vision without altering or removing corneal tissue. Often described as an "internal contact lens," it provides a proven, reversible alternative to laser vision correction—delivering high-definition visual quality while preserving the natural anatomy of your eye.

How ICL Surgery Works

Unlike corneal laser procedures (such as LASIK, PRK, or SMILE pro) that reshape the surface(cornea) of the eye, ICL surgery corrects your optical prescription from within:

Internal Lens Positioning: Through a micro-incision, the soft, flexible lens is placed in the posterior chamber—resting comfortably behind the iris (the colored part of the eye) and directly in front of your natural crystalline lens.

Corneal Preservation: Because no corneal tissue is removed, the structural integrity, natural curvature, and biomechanical strength of your cornea remain completely intact.

Invisible & Maintenance-Free: Once positioned, the lens is completely invisible to the naked eye and requires no daily care, cleaning, or maintenance.

Reversible Solution: Designed for permanent, long-term vision correction, the lens can nonetheless be safely removed or upgraded by an ophthalmic surgeon if your prescription changes significantly or if medical needs evolve in the future.

What Vision Problems Can ICL Correct?

Implantable Collamer Lenses offer broad treatment parameters, providing exceptional visual accuracy across a wide range of refractive errors:

Short-Sightedness (Myopia): Corrects mild to very high degrees of near-sightedness, including high prescriptions beyond the safe limit of corneal laser procedures.

Long-Sightedness (Hyperopia): Restores clear distance focus for farsighted eyes by adding internal focusing power.

Astigmatism: Toric ICL models correct irregular corneal curvature, providing sharp, undistorted vision.

Tailored Treatment Planning

The specific treatable range for your eyes depends on detailed diagnostic measurements—such as anterior chamber depth, white-to-white distance, and internal ocular anatomy. A thorough pre-operative assessment is essential to ensure adequate internal spacing and confirm your suitability for ICL implantation.

Who is a Good Candidate for ICL Surgery?

ICL surgery is not a one-size-fits-all procedure. Selecting the right candidate requires a precise evaluation of your prescription, internal ocular dimensions, and long-term eye health.

Why Patients Choose ICL

ICL is often the preferred vision correction solution for patients who seek maximum corneal preservation or fall outside standard laser parameters:

High Prescriptions: Ideal for patients with moderate to severe myopia, hyperopia, or astigmatism beyond the safe treatment limits of laser vision correction.

Thin or Irregular Corneas: Serves as a safe, effective option for patients whose corneal thickness or topography makes laser reshaping unsuitable.

Dry Eye Concerns: Because the procedure does not sever corneal nerves, it minimizes the risk of inducing or worsening dry eye symptoms.

Cornea-Sparing & Reversible: Appealing to individuals who prefer an additive procedure that preserves natural corneal architecture and can be safely removed or replaced if visual or medical needs change.

When ICL May Not Be Suitable

ICL surgery may not be recommended if clinical examination reveals:

Natural Lens Changes or Cataracts: Early lens opacities or significant presbyopic changes, where Refractive Lens Exchange (RLE) or cataract surgery is a more appropriate treatment.

Inadequate Internal Dimensions: An anterior chamber that is too shallow, or anatomical angles that restrict proper lens placement and fluid flow.

Glaucoma or Elevated Intraocular Pressure: Uncontrolled glaucoma, narrow angles, or structural factors that increase the risk of postoperative pressure spikes.

Unstable Prescription or Active Ocular Disease: Fluctuating refraction or active inflammatory ocular conditions.

Visual Outcomes & Long-Term Considerations of ICL

Implantable Collamer Lenses deliver exceptional visual accuracy and high-definition clarity. Understanding what to expect after surgery helps ensure a smooth, well-informed treatment experience:

Visual Outcomes & Freedom: The primary goal of ICL surgery is sharp, stable distance vision and reduced reliance on corrective eyewear. While many patients achieve complete visual independence for daily activities, some may still prefer light glasses for specific tasks or high-demand visual conditions.

Night Vision & Dysphotopsias: Some patients may experience minor halos, glare, or ring reflections around light sources in dim illumination or during night driving. Discussing your night-driving requirements during pre-operative planning ensures your optical expectations are aligned with your lifestyle.

Natural Age-Related Changes: An ICL provides long-term stability, but it does not alter the natural aging process of the eye. As you reach your mid-to-late 40s, presbyopia will naturally affect your close-focusing ability, requiring reading glasses for fine near work.

Future Cataract Management: Cataract formation is a normal part of aging. If cataracts develop later in life, the ICL can be safely removed during standard cataract surgery and replaced with an appropriate intraocular lens (IOL).

Refractive Lens Exchange (RLE)

Refractive Lens Exchange (RLE)—also known as Custom Lens Replacement—is a advanced vision correction procedure where the eye’s natural crystalline lens is replaced with a high-performance intraocular lens (IOL). While the surgical technique is identical to modern cataract surgery, RLE is performed primarily to correct refractive errors and presbyopia before a visually significant cataract forms.

How RLE Surgery Works

RLE corrects vision from within the eye by customizing the focusing power of the newly implanted lens to your lifestyle requirements:

Micro-Incision Access: Through a tiny, self-sealing incision, the natural lens is gently dissolved and removed using advanced ultrasound energy (phacoemulsification).

IOL Implantation: A custom artificial intraocular lens is carefully positioned inside the eye's natural capsular bag.

Corneal Preservation & Longevity: Unlike laser procedures, RLE does not reshape the cornea. Because the natural lens is replaced, you will never develop cataracts later in life, ensuring long-term optical stability.

What Vision Problems Can RLE Correct?

Refractive Lens Exchange provides a versatile, long-term solution across a comprehensive spectrum of optical errors, particularly for patients experiencing age-related focusing changes:

Presbyopia: Addresses the loss of near-focusing flexibility that occurs in your 40s and 50s. By selecting advanced multifocal or Extended Depth of Focus (EDOF) intraocular lenses, RLE can restore a continuous range of vision (near, intermediate, and distance).

Long-Sightedness (Hyperopia): Excellent for hyperopic patients, where laser vision correction may have structural limits or reduced long-term predictability.

Short-Sightedness (Myopia): Corrects moderate-to-high nearsightedness, performed following a thorough retinal health and peripheral vitreoretinal risk evaluation.

Astigmatism: Toric intraocular lenses neutralize corneal astigmatism to deliver crisp, clear visual focus.

Addressing Age-Related Near Blur

Presbyopia occurs when the eye’s natural lens loses its youthful elasticity, making close-up tasks like reading, working on digital screens, or checking a phone increasingly difficult. Because corneal laser surgery does not halt the aging of the natural lens, RLE is often the definitive choice for adults over 45 who want to address both their baseline prescription and age-related near blur simultaneously.

Who is a Good Candidate for Refractive Lens Exchange?

Refractive Lens Exchange (RLE) is an ideal option for mature patients seeking long-term visual independence from spectacles and contact lenses. candidacy depends on age, corneal health, lifestyle goals, and the presence of natural age-related lens changes.

Ideal Candidates Typically Include:

Adults Over 45 with Presbyopia: Individuals experiencing age-related loss of near-focusing flexibility who want a permanent solution for reading, digital screen work, and distance vision without reliance on multifocal or reading glasses.

Patients with Moderate to High Hyperopia or Myopia: Adults whose prescriptions—with or without astigmatism—fall outside the ideal treatment profile for corneal laser procedures or where lens replacement offers superior long-term stability.

Patients with Early Cataractous Changes: Individuals who show early signs of lens yellowing or clouding (incipient cataracts). Performing RLE removes the natural lens early, treating the refractive error and permanently eliminating the future need for cataract surgery.

When RLE May Not Be Suitable

RLE may not be recommended or may require additional specialized evaluation if pre-operative assessment reveals:

Younger Patients (Under 40–45): Younger individuals who retain natural accommodation (focusing flexibility) are generally better suited to cornea-preserving or additive options, such as SMILE pro, LASIK, PRK, or ICL.

Retinal Vulnerabilities: Patients with significant peripheral retinal pathology, lattice degeneration, or high axial myopia requiring detailed vitreoretinal clearance before proceeding.

Co-Existing Ocular Disease: Advanced glaucoma, macular degeneration, or severe dry eye syndrome that could compromise visual quality or limit the performance of specialized intraocular lenses.

A comprehensive pre-operative consultation and detailed ocular health assessment are essential to determine your candidacy and tailor the optimal intraocular lens platform to your daily visual needs and preferences.

Visual Outcomes & Long-Term Considerations of RLE

Refractive Lens Exchange delivers long-lasting visual improvement tailored to your specific lifestyle goals. Your visual outcome and degree of spectacle independence depend primarily on your starting prescription, overall ocular health, and the type of intraocular lens (IOL) selected:

Visual Outcomes & Lens Selection:

Monofocal Lenses: Set the focus for a single distance (typically clear distance vision). Reading glasses are generally required for intermediate screen work and near reading.

Multifocal & Extended Depth of Focus (EDOF) Lenses: Engineered to provide a broad range of vision (distance, intermediate, and near), significantly reducing overall reliance on glasses. Because these optics split or extend light pathways, some patients may notice mild trade-offs, such as subtle halos or reduced contrast in low-light environments. Your lens profile is selected based on your daily visual priorities and lighting conditions.

Spectacle Independence: While the objective of RLE is maximum freedom from glasses, some patients may still benefit from lightweight glasses for specific high-demand tasks—such as reading fine print in dim light, prolonged computer work, or specialized night driving.

Permanent Protection Against Cataracts: Because your natural crystalline lens is replaced during RLE, you can never develop cataracts in the treated eye. Your outcome remains structurally stable over time, though regular long-term ocular health checks remain important to monitor the retina and optic nerve as your eyes naturally age.
