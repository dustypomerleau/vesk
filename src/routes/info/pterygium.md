## What is a pterygium?

A pterygium is a fleshy triangular growth that develops on the conjunctiva or white part of the eye, typically on the side closest to the nose.
It is associated with environmental factors, espoiecially exposure to UV radiation. It is common in people who spend a lot of time outdoors in sunny and windy conditions, especially surfers. Hence, the condition’s other name – Surfer’s eye. It is also commonly seen in welders.

The condition is usually harmless if it is a small lesion. However, pterygium can grow progressive across the cornea and cause scarring and deterioration of vision. The growth can also change the shape of the cornea, causing vision problems due to astigmatism. They are not cancerous and are normally benign growths. Rarely, cancerous transformation on the surface of the eye can appear like a pterygium, hence would be important to have lesions assessed to ensure they are not cancerous.
One or both eyes can be affected, and commonly affects people aged between 20s and 50s.

Symptoms of a pterygium include localised redness or inflammation, irritation, itching, foreign body sensation and more seriously, corneal scarring. As it progresses, one can see a fleshy lesion start to extend into the coloured area of the eye. People with pterygium can also find it difficult to wear contact lenses in the affected eye due to the irregular ocular surface.

## Treatment

Treatment is considered if issues are caused by the pterygium, such as:

- Irritation and pain.
- Recurrent inflammation of the pterygium.
- Growth of the pterygium.
- Changing vision or deterioration of vision
- Prior to cataract surgery to achieve the best vision and refractive outcome.

In many instances, people chose to have surgery for cosmetic reasons.
Using modern micro-surgical techniques, pterygium surgery involves careful removal of the pterygium from the eye and cornea. The surface of the cornea that was affected is smoothed over so that it heals with minimal scarring. The area where the pterygium was excised is replaced with a graft of healthy conjunctival tissue taken from the same eye, and is fixed to the site with fine sutures or tissue glue. This minimises the recurrence of the pterygium in the future. The vision is usually unaffected in the immediate postoperative period and you would be able to continue with usual visual tasks such as driving and working on the computer. There is usually minimal discomfort after surgery, mild foreign body sensation may be experienced if sutures were used.

## How can pterygium be prevented?

Since it is caused by UV radiation, protecting your eyes from direct sunlight and wearing good quality sunglasses that meet Australian Standard and have a EPF UV rating or 9 or more would be beneficial.
Wear sunglasses when surfing or playing water sports.
Wear a wide brimmed hat when outside.
Wear protective glasses/goggles with UV protection when welding.
